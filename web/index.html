Method

What makes Today

Every hour we read the public news feeds of a fixed list of outlets and group the articles that cover the same event. A story makes Today only if outlets on both the left and the right are covering it. Every story that appears is kept in the archive.

Agreed

Only facts that at least one left-leaning and one right-leaning outlet state in their own words, and that no outlet we read contradicts. One side's reporting alone never makes this list.

Splits

Where the coverage tells it differently. Each side's account names the outlets that gave it; the words that differ are in bold. Where an account rests on a factual claim, that claim is checked.

Checks

Claims are checked by AI against primary sources: court records, official statements, government data. Our code confirms every quoted passage is on its source page. Doesn't hold up needs two different AI models to agree. Outlets are never evidence. No person reviews a check before it appears.

Loaded words

Charged or slanted words an outlet used in its own voice, not words it quoted. Each is confirmed to appear in that outlet's headline, summary or opening, with a plainer way to say it.

Who covered it

Dots place outlets by Pew Research Center's 2025 data on who gets news there: toward the left, Democrats are more likely than Republicans to; toward the right, the reverse. Outlets Pew did not survey are placed by their AllSides rating and drawn as squares. Each side counts equally, whatever its number of outlets.

Sources and privacy

Headlines are the outlets' own words. To write each summary we read the opening paragraphs of each article once; that text is never stored or republished. We link to every article. Pictures are freely licensed, from Wikimedia Commons, and credited. The code is public: github.com/hmiles1225/factual.

For publishers

Our scanner identifies itself as fACTUAL-scan and reads each feed once an hour. To ask a question or request a change, open an issue on GitHub.
